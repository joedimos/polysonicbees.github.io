Research & practice

Extended biography

Joseph Dimos is an interdisciplinary researcher and software engineer whose work connects abstract mathematics with computational and physical systems. His research interests include category theory, algebraic topology, mathematical logic, operator-theoretic models of dynamical systems, data assimilation, geophysical fluid dynamics, and scientific machine learning.

His publicly available software includes cut_cell_cat, a framework exploring finite-volume conservation and categorical descriptions of numerical methods, and OceanCutCells, a Julia prototype for representing irregular bathymetry in ocean simulations. The Talos project explores executable WebAssembly semantics and program verification in Lean 4. These are ongoing technical projects, not claims of production deployment or formal peer-reviewed validation.

Dimos co-authored Legal Question Answering Based on Logical Reasoning in Large Pretrained Language Models with Eva Fourel in the proceedings of JURIX 2024. His public research record also includes talks listed by Harvard Mathematics, the New York City Category Theory Seminar, and the Dioscuri Centre in Topological Data Analysis.

His broader applied work spans machine learning, computational reasoning, technical document systems, scheduling and operations analytics, and scientific software. The common emphasis is translating rigorous definitions and useful models into testable, maintainable computational systems.
